# Writer brief (one protocol)

You write all content files for one protocol of the Agentic Rails Atlas. Your protocol slug, its ATLAS_PROMPT.md section number and name are given in your task message. Today is 2026-10-11.

## Read first
1. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` sections 2, 5, 7, 12 and 13 (file layout, page template, STE components, writing rules, merge pipeline).
2. `src/lib/schemas.ts`: every file you write is validated against it at build time.
3. ATLAS_PROMPT.md Sections 2.3, 2.4, 2.6 (truth contract, copyright, style), 4.4 (section acceptance criteria), 9.3 (STE structure), 14.2 (coverage thresholds), Appendix D (disambiguation), and your protocol's Section 5.n in full.
4. Your dossier: `research/protocols/<slug>.md`, `<slug>.claims.json`, `<slug>.sources.json`. Use only claims present in the claims file. When you need a fact that is not there, verify it against a primary source (the cloned repositories in `research/vendor/` first), add the Source to your `sources.json` sidecar and the Claim to your `claims.json` with the right confidence, then use it.
5. The reference slice: `src/content/protocols/x402/` (when your protocol is not x402). Match its structure, depth and tone.
6. `src/content/glossary/terms.json` (or `research/glossary.draft.json` if the former is empty) so you link terms consistently, and `src/content/publications/publications.json` so you tag the right papers.

## Files to produce (all under `src/content/` unless noted)
- `protocols/<slug>/index.mdx`: frontmatter = `ProtocolMeta` (every field; `lastVerified` 2026-10-11; `keyFacts` rows cite claim ids; `mainFlow`, `stateMachine` and `diagrams` reference the ids you create; `playground.status` is `coming-next` unless the playground component already exists at `src/playgrounds/<slug>/Playground.tsx`). Body = section B practitioner text, 2,000 to 3,500 words, with these `###` subsections in order: Problem and design goals; Actors and roles; Core objects; Message flow step by step; Lifecycle and states; Security and trust model (summary); Versioning and governance; Extensibility; How it composes with the other seven; Limitations and open issues; What changed in 2025 and 2026. Add `<Mode eli5>` blocks (200 to 350 words in total, analogies allowed, no field names) at the top, and `<Mode spec>` blocks inside the relevant subsections with `<FieldTable>` tables (exact field names from the spec files), headers, enums, error codes and short wire examples labeled "Illustrative example, spec version X". Cite with `<Cite ids="clm-<slug>-NNN" />` after every factual sentence or table row; several ids may be space-separated. End the body with a `## Compare and compose` heading followed by 2 to 4 paragraphs that the section P component renders alongside the relations data. Import nothing: the components Mode, FieldTable, Cite and Callout are provided to the MDX by the layout.
- `protocols/<slug>/ste.mdx`: frontmatter `{protocol, title, lastVerified}`; body uses `<SteDescription>`, 2 to 4 `<SteProcedure title="...">` from different personas, 3 to 6 `<SteCaution>` and `<SteNote>` blocks, and one `<SteTerms>` list; 500 to 900 words; obeys the STE rules (procedural sentences at most 20 words, descriptive at most 25, imperative steps, active voice, no -ing verbs, no contractions, no semicolons). Run `pnpm lint:ste --protocol <slug>` until it passes.

- `protocols/<slug>/publication-tags.json`: `[{id, whyItMatters?}]` for 8 or more publications (direct, related or foundation) that section G should show; when no paper names the protocol, tag the closest work and let the section say so.

- `flows/<slug>-<name>.json`: the main happy-path flow (`mainFlow`) plus at least one failure or alternative-path flow, plus the protocol-specific sequences listed in Section 5.n "Diagrams"; every step with `detail`, `payload` where the spec defines a message (exact field names, `illustrative: true`), `crossesTrustBoundary`, `specRef` to the spec file or section, and `failures`.

- `diagrams/<id>.mmd` plus `<id>.json` (`DiagramMeta`) for: context (actors and trust boundaries), data model (core objects and their relations, `classDiagram` or `erDiagram`), composition with other protocols, the trust-boundary diagram for section H (may be the context diagram), and every extra listed in Section 5.n. Use Mermaid syntax only (flowchart, classDiagram, erDiagram, stateDiagram-v2, sequenceDiagram); keep labels short; no colors (the renderer themes them). Run `pnpm tsx scripts/flow-to-mermaid.ts && pnpm tsx scripts/render-diagrams.ts --only <id>` to check they render.

## Rules that fail the build
- No em dash (U+2014) anywhere. No banned phrases (`scripts/style/banned-phrases.json`). US spelling. Sentence-case headings. Dates as YYYY-MM-DD. Acronyms expanded on first use.
- Every number has an as-of date and a source. Every factual sentence has a `<Cite>`. Claims marked `verified` cite a primary source you (or the researcher) read. Anything you could not confirm is `reported` or `unverified`, never silently asserted.
- Never copy spec text; paraphrase and link. At most one quotation under 15 words per source across the site.
- Separate specified from deployed, announced from live, and name the spec version your field tables follow.
- The "Do not get wrong" items of Section 5.n are checked by an independent verifier; get them right and make them explicit in sections A and Q.

## Finish
Run `pnpm tsx scripts/verify/validate-content.ts`, `pnpm lint:style`, `pnpm lint:ste --protocol <slug>`, `pnpm verify:coverage --protocol <slug>` (after `pnpm merge` if it exists) and fix until they pass or the only remaining failures are ones you cannot fix with available sources (then add a waiver to `coverage-waivers.json` with the reason). Then `pnpm build` and confirm `dist/p/<slug>/index.html` exists. Final reply: counts (words in section B, ELI5 words, claims, sources, resources, publications tagged, diagrams, flows, threats, FAQ, quiz, flashcards), gate results, waivers, and the three facts you were least sure about.
