## The situation

A shopper asks Gemini for a pair of trail running shoes in size 10 under a set price, picks one, and pays with the card saved in Google Pay. The shopper is present for the whole purchase. The merchant, not Google, is the merchant of record and keeps the financial liability and the order.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-006" />

Two layers are involved, and they are not equally live. UCP-powered checkout began rolling out to US shoppers in AI Mode in Search and the Gemini app on 2026-02-11, starting with Etsy and Wayfair items.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-174" /> The AP2 mandate layer is specified inside UCP as an optional extension.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-193" /> Google said on 2026-05-19 that it would begin bringing AP2 to its products in the coming months, starting with Gemini Spark.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-169" /> Production use of AP2 mandates is unverified anywhere, including inside UCP, as of 2026-10-11.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-139" /> Read the AP2 steps below as "per spec; live use unverified".

## Actors

- **Shopper**, present and approving.
- **Gemini**, the UCP platform and, in AP2 terms, the Shopping Agent.
- **Trusted checkout UI**, the AP2 Trusted Surface, which must be non-agentic.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-041" />
- **Google Pay**, the credential provider, whose handler `com.google.pay` is a specification authored by the provider.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-051" />
- **Merchant**, the UCP business, which publishes its profile at `/.well-known/ucp`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-054" />
- **Merchant PSP**, the AP2 Merchant Payment Processor, plus the card network and issuer.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-039" />

## Step by step

1. **Ask (no protocol).** The shopper describes what they want.
2. **Discover (UCP).** Gemini reads the merchant profile; it requires a `ucp` object with `version`, `services` and `payment_handlers`, and signing keys sit in a top-level `keys` array.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-055 clm-ucp-058" /> A merchant that accepts AP2 lists `dev.ucp.common.payment.ap2_mandate` there.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-106" />
3. **Create checkout (UCP).** Gemini calls `POST /checkout-sessions` with its profile URI in `UCP-Agent`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-122 clm-ucp-088" /> Google says requests from its surfaces carry `UCP-Agent` and a User-Agent beginning with `Google/UCP`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-157" /> If AP2 is in the capability intersection, the session is locked and neither side may fall back to an unprotected checkout.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-109" />
4. **Signed checkout (UCP AP2 extension).** The merchant must include `ap2.merchant_authorization` in every checkout response, a JWS with detached content over the checkout without the `ap2` field.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-110 clm-ucp-083" />
5. **Verify and hand off (AP2).** Gemini must verify the merchant signature before showing the checkout.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-111" />
6. **Approve (AP2).** In the human-present flow the user approves the closed Checkout and Payment Mandates on a Trusted Surface.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-087" />
7. **Get the credential (UCP handler).** The credential provider defines the handler, the merchant configures it, and the platform executes it to obtain a token.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-053" /> On Google AI surfaces UCP checkout supports only `PAN_ONLY` for Google Pay `allowed_auth_methods`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-192" />
8. **Create the closed mandates (AP2, per spec).** The Checkout Mandate requires `vct`, `checkout_jwt` and `checkout_hash`.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-050" /> The Payment Mandate requires `vct`, `transaction_id`, `payee`, `payment_amount` and `payment_instrument`, and `transaction_id` is the hash of `checkout_jwt`, which binds payment to one checkout.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-058 clm-ap2-060" />
9. **Complete (UCP).** The checkout mandate goes in `ap2.checkout_mandate`, an SD-JWT with key binding, and the payment mandate in `payment.instruments[*].credential.token`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-084 clm-ucp-085" /> UCP signs requests with RFC 9421 and binds bodies with an RFC 9530 `Content-Digest`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-133" />
10. **Merchant verifies (AP2).** The merchant checks that the hash of the checkout JWT it sent matches `checkout_hash`.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-201" />
11. **Processor verifies (AP2).** The Merchant Payment Processor checks that the credential is scoped to this checkout, for example by finding the closed Payment Mandate inside it.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-200" />
12. **Authorize (card rails).** The merchant's PSP must be able to process Google's tokens; the merchant does not need Google Pay on its own site.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-189" />
13. **Order and receipts (UCP, AP2).** Complete returns `completed` with an order.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-100" /> Per AP2 the processor returns a signed Payment Receipt and the merchant a signed Checkout Receipt.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-090" />
14. **Order webhooks (UCP).** The merchant posts full order snapshots to the platform's `config.webhook_url` with `Webhook-Id` and `Webhook-Timestamp`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-081 clm-ucp-114" /> Webhooks must be signed with RFC 9421, and the platform must reject events whose signer does not match the order's business.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-115" />

## Which protocol does what

| Steps | Protocol | Object or message | Spec version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 to 4 | UCP | profile, `POST /checkout-sessions`, `ap2.merchant_authorization` | v2026-08-25 |
| 5 to 6, 8 | AP2 | Trusted Surface, `mandate.checkout.1`, `mandate.payment.1` | v0.2 (per spec) |
| 7 | UCP handler | `com.google.pay`, `PAN_ONLY` | Google handler |
| 9 | UCP | `complete`, `ap2.checkout_mandate`, `credential.token` | v2026-08-25 |
| 10 to 11, 13 | AP2 | mandate verification, Checkout and Payment Receipts | v0.2 (per spec) |
| 12 | Card rails | authorization | outside both protocols |
| 14 | UCP | order webhook, RFC 9421 | v2026-08-25 |

AP2 v0.2 was released on 2026-04-28.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-027" /> The UCP release that renamed the AP2 extension to `dev.ucp.common.payment.ap2_mandate` is v2026-08-25.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-030" />

## Where controls apply

- **Before authorization.** The platform allowlists businesses and capability namespaces and checks the advertised handlers before it creates a checkout.<Cite ids="src-ucp-overview" /> UCP has no spending cap field; caps belong to AP2 mandates or the credential provider.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-151" />
- **At signing.** Consent binds to one merchant-signed checkout. The checkout mandate contains the full checkout including `ap2.merchant_authorization`, so the platform's signature covers the merchant's.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-112" /> Without AP2, the agent must hand the checkout to a trusted, deterministic UI for the user to review and place the order.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-097" />
- **At the edge.** With API keys, OAuth or mTLS, the merchant must confirm that the caller may act for the profile in `UCP-Agent`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-143" /> Replay protection rests on a signed `Idempotency-Key`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-136" />
- **At settlement.** PSP fraud screening applies, and 3DS stays with the business, handler and PSP rather than in UCP messages.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-108" /> AP2 v0.2 specifies no step-up; a failed check falls back through `unresolved_constraint`.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-118" />
- **After the fact.** AP2 dispute verification recomputes the `checkout_jwt` hash and checks both receipt references against the closed mandates.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-097" /> Order adjustments can record disputes, but handling them stays with the merchant and its PSP.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-154" />

## What is observable from outside

Business profiles are public and cacheable, so anyone can see the capabilities, transports, payment handlers and keys a domain advertises, including whether it accepts AP2.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-155" /> The rest is private. Checkout sessions, payment credentials, mandates and webhooks flow point to point.<Cite ids="src-ucp-overview" /> AP2 defines no public registry, explorer or event stream.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-131" /> Google keeps the order data shown on the shopper's My orders page until the shopper asks for deletion.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-161" />

## What can go wrong

- **Unsigned or altered checkout.** A missing or invalid merchant signature yields `merchant_authorization_missing` or `merchant_authorization_invalid`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-086" /> No order is placed and nobody pays.
- **Mandate problems.** A missing mandate yields `mandate_required`; an expired, mis-scoped or badly signed one yields `mandate_expired`, `mandate_scope_mismatch` or `mandate_invalid_signature`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-086" />
- **Lost complete response.** During `complete_in_progress` the platform must not start another update or complete.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-101" />
- **Escalation.** With `requires_escalation` the business must provide `continue_url`, and the platform must send the shopper there.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-098 clm-ucp-099" />
- **Wrong item or price.** The merchant is merchant of record and carries the order; the shopper accepted the merchant's terms during checkout.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-162" /> AP2 leaves liability to the payment networks.<Cite ids="clm-ap2-120" />
- **Forged webhook.** The platform must reject events whose signer is not the order's business.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-115" />

Costs: Google publishes no fee schedule; because the merchant uses its own PSP, its normal processing fees presumably apply, which is an inference, not a statement from Google.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-190" /> Google told The Register it does not plan to take a cut of sales.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-188" />

## Variants

- **A network token instead of a card on file.** UCP defines a `network_token` credential with `number` and `cryptogram`.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-076" /> On Google surfaces, though, a network-token path through the Google Pay handler is not documented, because only `PAN_ONLY` is supported there.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-192" /> Which network's agentic token, if any, could flow inside a Google Pay handler is not published.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-204" />
- **Without AP2, as documented today.** Drop steps 5, 8, 10 and 11: the checkout goes to a trusted UI for review, and UCP's own signatures and idempotency carry the integrity.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-097" /> Google also offers approved merchants an embedded checkout as an alternative to native checkout.<Cite ids="clm-ucp-179" />
