## What it is

These standards let an issuer sign claims about a subject, let the holder keep them in a wallet, and let a verifier check them later, ideally with only the needed claims shown. Two encodings matter here. The W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 became a Recommendation on 2025-05-15, as part of a family of VC 2.0 specifications W3C announced that day. <Cite ids="src-gloss-vc-2 src-orbit-kya-w3c-vc2-press" /> Decentralized identifiers (DIDs) give issuers and holders resolvable identifiers. DID Core 1.0 has been a Recommendation since 2022-07-19, and DID 1.1 is a Candidate Recommendation Snapshot of 2026-03-05. <Cite ids="src-gloss-did-1 src-orbit-kya-did-1-1" />

The other encoding comes from the IETF. SD-JWT, published as RFC 9901 in November 2025, makes individual JWT claims selectively disclosable and can bind the token to a holder key. <Cite ids="src-mcap-rfc9901" /> SD-JWT VC defines a credential format on top of it. The IESG opened its evaluation with an approval ballot on 2026-10-27 and discussed the draft on its 2026-11-05 telechat; the authors then posted revisions up to `draft-ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc-27` (2026-11-16), and on 2026-11-17 the last open DISCUSS position moved to No Objection, which left the ballot at 1 Yes and 11 No Objection. <Cite ids="src-orbit-kya-sd-jwt-vc-draft" /> On 2026-11-21 the draft was still in IESG evaluation, waiting on the area director's follow-up, so it is not approved and not yet an RFC. <Cite ids="src-orbit-kya-sd-jwt-vc-draft" />

Presentation is the last step. OpenID for Verifiable Presentations (OpenID4VP) 1.0 became a final OpenID Foundation specification on 2025-07-09. <Cite ids="src-gloss-openid4vp" /> The W3C Digital Credentials API, which lets a website ask the browser to request a credential from a wallet, is a Working Draft. The current one is dated 2026-11-19 (as of 2026-11-21), after Working Drafts of 2026-09-04, 2026-10-15, 2026-10-20, 2026-10-29 and 2026-11-05. <Cite ids="src-orbit-dc-api-wd-20261119 src-orbit-dc-api-wd-20261105 src-gloss-dc-api" />

## How it relates to the eight

[AP2](/p/ap2/) v0.2 specifies OpenID4VP with SD-JWT VCs. Delegation travels in OpenID4VP `transaction_data` of type `delegate`, the Digital Credentials API is recommended, and mandates carry `vct` values `mandate.checkout.1` and `mandate.payment.1`, chained with a delegate SD-JWT. AP2 does not reference the VC Data Model or DIDs, so those are the wider family rather than AP2 dependencies. <Cite ids="src-ap2-agent-authorization src-ap2-delegate-sd-jwt" /> AP2 v0.2 cites Delegate SD-JWT as the individual draft `draft-gco-oauth-delegate-sd-jwt-00`. The IETF OAuth working group adopted it on 2026-10-30 as `draft-ietf-oauth-delegate-sd-jwt-00`, which replaces the individual draft, adds C. Bormann (SPRIND) as a co-author and is still a draft, not an RFC. The working group text renames the type of a chained token that carries its own key binding from `kb+sd-jwt+kb` to `kb-sd-jwt+kb`. <Cite ids="src-ap2-delegate-sd-jwt-wg src-ap2-delegate-sd-jwt" /> The authors' slides for the OAuth working group at IETF 127 on 2026-11-18 keep domain-specific policy, such as payment limits and line items, out of the draft's scope. <Cite ids="src-orbit-delegate-sd-jwt-ietf127-slides src-orbit-oauth-ietf127-minutes" />

Verifiable Intent, the intent layer of [Mastercard Agent Pay](/p/mastercard-agent-pay/), uses SD-JWT per RFC 9901 with key-binding chains (`typ` values `sd+jwt`, `kb-sd-jwt` and `kb-sd-jwt+kb`). Its design notes call it a credential format and say a future guide may define presentation over OpenID4VP. <Cite ids="src-mcap-vi-format src-mcap-vi-rationale" /> Outside the eight, Catena's Agent Commerce Kit v1 uses DIDs and VCs, and its open v2 proposal moves away from VCs toward plain JOSE grants. <Cite ids="src-orbit-kya-ack-repo src-orbit-kya-ack-v2-rfc" />
