<Mode eli5>

Picture a big shop with a guard at the door. Many robots come to the shop. Some robots shop for real people. Others copy the price tags or try to cheat. The guard cannot tell them apart by looking, so he sends most robots away.

Visa checks the stamp of each good robot and keeps a book of stamp shapes at its office. Each time a good robot walks in, it stamps a fresh note: which shop and which shelf it wants, the time, a code for this visit, and one word that says "just looking" or "I want to pay". The note is good for a few minutes only. The guard, or a security company the shop hires, compares the stamp with Visa's book. If the stamp matches and the note is fresh, the robot may come in.

At the till the robot can hand over two more stamped envelopes. One says "this is a customer you may know" without saying the person's name or email. The other holds what the shop needs to get paid: a check that the card details the robot types are right, a locked box that only the shop can open, or a note that says "I owe you for this page".

The stamp pays for nothing. It only says who the robot is and what it wants to do. The money still moves the normal card way, and the shopper's permission to spend lives in another Visa service.

</Mode>

Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP) answers one question for a merchant. Is this automated request from an AI (artificial intelligence) agent that a payment network has approved, and does the agent want to browse or to pay?<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-001 clm-visa-tap-031" /> The agent signs each HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol) request with RFC (Request for Comments) 9421 HTTP Message Signatures and puts a tag in the signature: `agent-browser-auth` for browsing, `agent-payer-auth` for checkout and payment.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-037 clm-visa-tap-040" /> The merchant, or a CDN (content delivery network) in front of it, fetches the agent's public key from the network's key directory.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-075 clm-visa-tap-124" /> It checks a validity window of at most 8 minutes and a nonce, rebuilds the signed text and verifies the signature.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-075 clm-visa-tap-043" /> At checkout the same key can sign two JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) objects in the request body, one that helps the merchant recognize the consumer and one that carries payment data.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-078 clm-visa-tap-047 clm-visa-tap-056" /> What TAP does not give you: a payment, the user's consent, spending limits or a liability rule. The money moves through Visa Intelligent Commerce tokens and ordinary card processing, and the user's approval lives there too.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-002 clm-visa-tap-129 clm-visa-tap-169" /> TAP is a Visa specification under Visa's own terms, not an IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force) standard.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-003" />

### Problem and design goals

Merchants and the services that protect their sites have long classed automated traffic as bots and blocked most of it, and an agent shopping for a real cardholder arrives as exactly that kind of traffic.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-236" /> Visa's launch release quoted Adobe data (dated August 2025) that AI-driven traffic to US retail sites had risen more than 4,700 percent over the past year.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-172" /> Visa said on 2025-11-20 that malicious bot-initiated transactions had risen 25 percent over six months, and 40 percent in the US.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-173" /> Neither figure measures TAP traffic; they describe the problem TAP was built for.

Visa announced TAP on 2025-10-14, and said it was developed with Cloudflare. Its overview page presented TAP as an enhancement to Visa Intelligent Commerce (VIC), the agent payment program Visa unveiled on 2025-04-30.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-004 clm-visa-tap-005 clm-visa-tap-007 clm-visa-tap-008" /> The specification is written for the case where the merchant does not know the agent in advance, and a merchant that has not adopted it can ignore every signature.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-233" /> It names four goals for the agent signature. The first two are strong identity tied to a payment scheme's program and requests that cannot be altered unnoticed. The other two are replay protection through timestamps and nonces, and no reliance on IP addresses or extra header content.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-223" /> A key that belongs to Visa implies that the agent takes part in, and complies with, the VIC program.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-224" />

That overview page, retired in October 2026, grouped what an agent can prove into three kinds of information: agent intent, consumer recognition and payment information.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-225" /> The specification turns them into three signatures, one in the request headers and two in the request body.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-227" /> It covers two moments of a purchase. Browsing means asking about a product, its availability and its final cost after taxes and shipping; payment means buying it or paying for access to a resource.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-237" />

### Actors and roles

The specification names eight participants: Agent Providers, Checkout Enablers, Consumers, Site Protection Providers, Identity Providers, Merchants, Payment Schemes and a Key Store.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-030" /> An Agent Provider is a certified AI platform that a payment scheme has onboarded and approved to pay on behalf of its account holders.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-031" /> Visa's rules require such a provider to enroll in VIC and register with Visa, but the onboarding and key-registration steps are not public.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-125" /> The retired overview page said the guide for generating signatures went only to onboarded agents.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-087" />

A Checkout Enabler is an intermediary that an agent may use to reach merchant sites, and TAP expects only one thing of it: pass the message signatures and query parameters through unchanged.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-032" /> A Site Protection Provider is the layer in front of the merchant's site, such as a CDN, a trust management system or a proxy.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-033" /> Verification can happen at the merchant or at that provider on the merchant's behalf.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-035" /> Edge providers offer to do this work: Cloudflare published its TAP checks on 2025-10-24, Akamai announced TAP support across Akamai Cloud on 2025-12-17, and Visa said on 2026-07-02 that both support implementation.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-085 clm-visa-tap-151 clm-visa-tap-152" />

The Merchant does not have to use TAP and, the specification says, does not necessarily have to register with a payment scheme to accept its credentials.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-034" /> A merchant that recognizes agents may serve them simpler pages or a shorter checkout.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-234" /> The Payment Scheme, Visa in this case, sets the rules for agent-initiated transactions and hosts the key store at a well-known URL.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-030" /> The specification named `https://mcp.visa.com/.well-known/jwks` until October 2026 and names no URL today.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-069" /> For VIC, Visa also acts as the Identity Provider: it signs the ID Token that the consumer object carries.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-050" /> The Consumer signs nothing in TAP; their approval of a purchase happens earlier, inside VIC, with a Visa Payment Passkey or another issuer-approved method.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-141" />

### Core objects

TAP has three signed things. The first is the agent recognition signature: two RFC 9421 header fields, `Signature-Input` with the metadata and `Signature` with the value.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-037" /> Its minimum covered components are `@authority` and `@path`, and it must carry six parameters: `created`, `expires`, `keyid`, `alg`, `nonce` and `tag`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-038 clm-visa-tap-039" /> The two timestamps may be at most 8 minutes apart.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-043" /> The nonce is unusual: TAP calls it a session identifier; multiple message signatures in one header should share it, and each signed body object repeats it.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-042" /> Agents and payment schemes may add fields beyond this minimum.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-046" />

The second is the Agentic Consumer Recognition Object, `agenticConsumer`, in the request body. It holds at least a `nonce`, an `idToken`, a `contextualData` object, a `kid`, an `alg` and a `signature`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-047" /> The `contextualData` object carries a country code, a postal code, the consumer's IP address and device data, the last two collected when the consumer set up the Payment Instruction in VIC.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-048 clm-visa-tap-049" /> The ID Token is a JWT (JSON Web Token) compatible with an OpenID Connect ID Token and signed by Visa as a JWS (JSON Web Signature).<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-050" /> Its header needs `alg`, `kid` and `typ`, and the `none` algorithm is not allowed.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-051" /> Its contact claims are obfuscated, so a merchant that wants to match a returning customer keeps its own mapping table from obfuscated email or phone values to its account holders.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-054 clm-visa-tap-055" /> Until October 2026 Visa's overview page also listed a blockchain wallet address as possible consumer data, a field the specification's tables never defined; Visa's current TAP pages do not mention it.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-232" />

The third is the Agentic Payment Container, `agenticPaymentContainer`, also in the body, with at least `nonce`, `kid`, `alg` and `signature`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-056" /> What else it holds depends on how the merchant takes payment. It can carry a hash of the card credentials for a key-entered guest checkout, `cardMetadata` for display, or an encrypted `payload` for merchants that process by API (application programming interface). It can also carry a `browsingIOU` after the merchant answered with HTTP 402.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-057" /> The `cardMetadata` object includes the last four digits and the payment account reference (PAR), which lets a merchant link token and virtual card payments to one funding account.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-058" /> The `payload` is encrypted to a merchant public key that the merchant onboards to each payment scheme, and it carries a Token object, `dynamicData`, addresses and consumer contact details.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-061 clm-visa-tap-231" />

Both body objects are signed with the same private key as the header signature and carry the same nonce, which is what links them to the message signature.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-078" /> TAP publishes no JSON Schema, OpenAPI description or test vectors for its objects, and the sample repository implements neither body object.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-064 clm-visa-tap-065" /> The field tables below therefore follow the specification page itself, which carries no version number.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-023" />

### Message flow step by step

The main sequence in section C follows one shopping trip: a signed browsing request, then a signed checkout request with both body objects, verified at a CDN and then at the merchant.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-237 clm-visa-tap-035" /> TAP defines verification steps for the merchant and a key retrieval call, not a checkout or payment API; the agent side lives in Visa's non-public client guide.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-075 clm-visa-tap-070 clm-visa-tap-087" />

1. The agent sends `GET` for a product page with `Signature-Input` and `Signature` headers covering `@authority` and `@path`, tagged `agent-browser-auth`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-038 clm-visa-tap-040" /> A Checkout Enabler on the way is expected to pass the signatures and query parameters through unchanged.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-032" />
2. The verifier looks for a signature tagged `agent-browser-auth` or `agent-payer-auth`. With neither tag, the request was not signed by a trusted agent, and the merchant may block it or judge it by other means.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-041" />
3. The verifier checks that every minimum field is present, that `created` is in the past and `expires` in the future, and that the two are at most 8 minutes apart.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-075 clm-visa-tap-043" />
4. If the verifier keeps the nonces of the last 8 minutes, it blocks a nonce it has already seen.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-077" />
5. It takes the public key for `keyid` from its cache or from the key store; a key that cannot be found or has expired blocks the request.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-076 clm-visa-tap-128" />
6. It rebuilds the signature base and verifies the signature. Any failure in steps 3 to 6 blocks the request.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-076" /> On success the merchant treats the caller as a trusted agent that is browsing or paying, and may still limit what it can do.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-075" />
7. At checkout the agent sends the same kind of signature with `agent-payer-auth`, plus `agenticConsumer` and `agenticPaymentContainer` in the body, all with the same nonce and key.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-040 clm-visa-tap-078 clm-visa-tap-228" />
8. The merchant checks each body object: required fields, a nonce equal to the header nonce, the key for `kid`, and a signature over the other fields in the order received.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-079 clm-visa-tap-081" />
9. The merchant processes the payment data. For a key-entered card, it compares its own hash of the card with the credential hash and declines on a mismatch.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-060" /> For an encrypted `payload`, the merchant decrypts it with its private key, and for a `browsingIOU` it checks the IOU against its 402 response.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-061 clm-visa-tap-083" /> Authorization then runs through the merchant's acquirer as for any card payment; TAP adds nothing there.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-002 clm-visa-tap-170" />

The two body objects fail differently. Consumer data that fails verification may be inaccurate, and the merchant may still use it.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-080" /> Payment data with missing fields, a nonce mismatch or a bad signature should not be used for payment.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-081" /> Yet a missing key only makes it possibly inaccurate, and the same section leaves usability to the merchant's discretion.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-219 clm-visa-tap-229" /> The nonce comparison may be skipped when a payment is requested without a message signature.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-082" />

Cloudflare's edge runs the same checks in its own order: headers present, key fetched by `keyid`, time window, nonce unused, tag allowed, signature base rebuilt, Ed25519 verification.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-085" /> The same post's example request carries `tag="web-bot-auth"`, which its own tag check would reject; follow the Visa specification.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-086" /> Section C has the decision tree and three failure paths: an untagged request, an expired window and a replayed nonce.

### Lifecycle and states

TAP has no lifecycle objects of its own, no order, mandate or token. The state it relies on sits with the parties: the verifier's optional nonce record and key cache, and the merchant's record of a 402 it issued.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-089" /> The merchant also keeps its mapping table for ID Token contact values, and the agent keeps the IOU sequence counter.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-089" /> The state machine in section C therefore follows one request through verification, from received to served, blocked or untrusted.

The window is short on purpose. A signature lives at most 8 minutes, and the sample agent sets `expires` to exactly `created` plus 8 minutes with a random UUID as nonce.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-043 clm-visa-tap-101" /> Because the specification calls the nonce a session identifier shared across signatures, a verifier that blocks every repeated nonce could also block a legitimate second signature in the same session.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-042 clm-visa-tap-077" /> The text does not resolve this.

The purchase itself has a lifecycle, but it lives in VIC. Visa's API client enrolls a card with `POST /vacp/v1/cards`, creates, updates and cancels purchase instructions under `/vacp/v1/instructions`, fetches payment credentials with `POST /vacp/v1/instructions/{instructionId}/credentials` and reports outcomes with `POST /vacp/v1/instructions/{instructionId}/confirmations`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-142 clm-visa-tap-143" /> Enrolling a card in VIC provisions an agent-specific token, followed by issuer step-up where required and passkey setup.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-140" /> The consumer then authenticates each purchase instruction, with a Visa Payment Passkey or another issuer-approved method, before the agent fetches credentials.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-141" /> Section C shows that link as its own sequence.

### Security and trust model (summary)

<Mode eli5>

The stamp proves the robot is one Visa knows, and the short time limit stops anyone from copying an old note. But the note only names the shop and the shelf, not what the robot asks for there, and the guard does not have to keep a list of codes already used.

</Mode>

TAP's guarantee is narrow: a request signed with a key from the scheme's store, for this host and path, inside an 8-minute window, with a stated purpose.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-124 clm-visa-tap-038 clm-visa-tap-126" /> Trust rests on two things the public cannot inspect: Visa's onboarding of agents and how Visa admits and distributes agent keys.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-125 clm-visa-tap-074" />

The gaps are specific. The minimum signature does not cover the method, the query string or a content digest, so the body, including both signed objects' transport, is not bound to the header signature.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-117 clm-visa-tap-095" /> The Web Bot Auth draft warns that a signature over the authority alone can be reused against any method, path or body until it expires.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-217" /> The specification's own benefits text claims coverage of method and headers that its minimum does not require.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-226" /> Nonce tracking is optional, so replay protection depends on the verifier keeping state.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-127" /> Body objects are signed over all fields in the order received, with no named canonicalization and no test vector.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-079 clm-visa-tap-028" /> The credential hash has no stated algorithm or salt, and the data it hashes (a 16-digit number, expiry and a 3-digit code) has little entropy.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-118 clm-visa-tap-059" />

The published material does not hold together in places. The JWKS that the specification named until October 2026 held one 2048-bit RSA key from a Visa sandbox CA (certificate authority), with no `alg` or `use`, and no Ed25519 key, on 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-19.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-071 clm-visa-tap-072" /> So the specification's example `keyid` cannot be resolved there. In Visa's own code that key encrypts tokens for Visa's MCP server, and nothing public confirms the JWKS as a store of TAP agent keys.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-239" /> The example `keyid`, nonce, timestamps and signature match a test vector in the Web Bot Auth draft that signs a different base with a different tag.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-114" /> The browsing and checkout examples also show the same signature, so neither example can verify as printed.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-116" /> The example window is 3,600 seconds against the 8-minute rule.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-115" /> No CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures) entry (NVD keyword search) or GitHub advisory (repository list and advisory database) named TAP on 2026-10-19.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-119 clm-visa-tap-120" /> The formal analysis of agent payment protocols by Jiang et al. (arXiv 2609.00060) did not model it.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-121" /> Section H has the threat table.

### Versioning and governance

There is no version number. The specification page shows no version or date, the Product Terms are dated 2025-10-10, and the sample repository has six commits from 2025-10-12 to 2025-10-28 and no tags or releases.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-023 clm-visa-tap-012 clm-visa-tap-024" /> Nothing has been merged since; twelve later pull requests are open or were closed unmerged.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-026" /> On 2026-10-19 Visa's API reference also listed a Trusted Agent Protocol entry labeled v1, with one sandbox endpoint and no schemas; the specification itself remains unversioned.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-241" />

Visa governs TAP alone. The Product Terms grant a nonexclusive, nontransferable and revocable license, conditioned on implementing the current specification in full and in strict conformance.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-013 clm-visa-tap-014" /> They bar modifications to the specification, allow the sample code to be adapted only as needed for applications that implement the current specification, and assign unpermitted modifications to Visa.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-015" /> Licensees must apply each Visa update at least yearly and within six months of its publication, though production use needs no Solution Approval review.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-016 clm-visa-tap-017" /> The implementer alone carries disclosure and consent duties toward users.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-018" /> The sample repository's LICENSE.md points to these terms while its source files carry MIT-style permission text, and GitHub reports no recognized license.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-019 clm-visa-tap-020 clm-visa-tap-021" />

Visa calls TAP an open framework and says it will align with the IETF, the OpenID Foundation and EMVCo, with the first specifications applying to the Visa network in this phase.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-203 clm-visa-tap-010 clm-visa-tap-011" /> None of those bodies publishes TAP: Visa publishes it under its own terms, and EMVCo's draft agentic framework of 2026-09-01 did not name it when read on 2026-10-11.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-003 clm-visa-tap-244" />

### Extensibility

The specification is a framework with a Visa implementation inside it, and the extension points are loose. Agents and schemes may add signature fields beyond the minimum.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-046" /> The consumer object may carry a loyalty number or another party's identity token, and the payment container's content changes with the merchant's processing path.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-047 clm-visa-tap-057" /> The specification says agents can talk over web or API channels and protocols such as MCP (Model Context Protocol) and ACP (Agentic Commerce Protocol), but it publishes no binding for them.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-092" /> A shared key store across payment schemes is named as a goal; for now Visa runs its own.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-205" />

### How it composes with the other seven

TAP is an identity layer that sits in front of checkout and payment protocols. Visa authored the card method of Stripe and Tempo's MPP (Machine Payments Protocol), and says that method and its SDK (software development kit) are built on VIC and TAP.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-185 clm-visa-tap-183" /> The `mppx` SDK can sign and verify TAP request attestations.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-162" /> The `browsingIOU` answers an HTTP 402 but uses none of x402's headers or payloads, while Visa sits on the x402 Foundation as a Premier member.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-190 clm-visa-tap-188" /> Visa said at launch that it was working with partners so that TAP would complement ACP, which OpenAI and Stripe maintain.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-192" /> Intelligent Commerce Connect, in pilot since 2026-04-08, accepts TAP, MPP, ACP and UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol) payments, but no wire-level binding to ACP or UCP is published.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-154 clm-visa-tap-193 clm-visa-tap-195" /> TAP has no documented link to AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol) or ERC-8004.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-197 clm-visa-tap-202" /> Section P goes further.

### Limitations and open issues

- **Proprietary and revocable.** The terms bar modifying the specification, limit sample-code changes to conforming applications and can be withdrawn, which limits independent implementations and forks.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-013 clm-visa-tap-015" />
- **One directory per network.** Visa runs its own key directory and Mastercard's guide points to a separate one, so a verifier needs one integration per network.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-123 clm-visa-tap-205 clm-visa-tap-221" />
- **Key store mismatch.** The JWKS (JSON Web Key Set) that the specification used to name does not hold the kind of key the examples use, and Visa has not said how production agent keys reach verifiers.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-071 clm-visa-tap-239 clm-visa-tap-074" />
- **Spec quality.** No version, no changelog, "RCF 9421" for RFC 9421, inconsistent field spellings, a Python sample whose signature parameters start with the label and leave out `alg`, and no test vectors.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-204 clm-visa-tap-068 clm-visa-tap-230 clm-visa-tap-064" />
- **Sample drift.** The sample uses the unregistered algorithm name `rsa-pss-sha256`, adds spaces to the serialized parameters, and builds a non-RFC base in the merchant backend.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-097 clm-visa-tap-098 clm-visa-tap-099" /> Its CDN proxy logs the tag without checking it, enforces no 8-minute limit (it rejects only expired signatures and `created` times more than 60 seconds ahead), and protects only `/product/` paths.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-100 clm-visa-tap-088" />
- **Privacy.** Every merchant the agent visits can receive the consumer's IP address, device data, country and postal code, and the specification sets no minimization or retention rule.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-136 clm-visa-tap-137" />
- **Unclear transport.** The README says identifiers arrive through query parameters, the specification puts them in the body, and nothing says how a body travels on a `GET`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-091" />
- **Who carries the loss.** Worldpay wrote on 2026-07-30 that no liability shift exists yet for agentic transactions, though fraud on properly tokenized agent payments generally follows existing rules.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-169 clm-visa-tap-170" /> Visa's rules govern Visa and its member institutions and yield to applicable law. Since 2025-10-18 they have made the cardholder responsible, within Visa's system, for what an Agentic Payment Provider does in an Agentic Transaction, as if the cardholder had initiated it. Visa calls none of its agent rules a liability shift.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-171" /> Issuers must pay for Agentic Transactions made under the cardholder's payment instructions and must not block them wholesale. From 2026-10-24 a provider's login ID also counts among the evidence that can defeat a card-absent fraud dispute.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-238" />
- **Open questions in the repository** ask what standing a payment instruction keeps after its agent is revoked and how receipts should be signed.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-206" />

## Compare and compose

Mastercard Agent Pay is the closest sibling. Cloudflare describes both as building on Web Bot Auth with a tag in `Signature-Input` and a required nonce, verified by the same edge checks.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-199 clm-visa-tap-085" /> The details differ: Mastercard's guide gives one tag, `Agent-pay-auth`, where Visa has two, and Cloudflare said each network would host its own key directory.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-220 clm-visa-tap-123" /> Neither Mastercard's guide nor Cloudflare's post gives a working Mastercard directory location: the only host in the guide did not resolve on 2026-10-11 or 2026-10-19.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-221" /> Both networks also say "agentic token", for different products: Visa's agentic network tokens in VIC, Mastercard's Agentic Tokens in Agent Pay.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-201" />

Against the IETF Web Bot Auth draft, TAP is aligned in direction and different in detail. The draft requires the tag `web-bot-auth`, a covered `Signature-Agent` header and a `keyid` that is a JWK thumbprint.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-108 clm-visa-tap-109 clm-visa-tap-110" /> It finds keys through the `Signature-Agent` URL, by default a directory at `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-112" /> TAP uses its own tags and does not require `Signature-Agent`. It requires a nonce, caps the window at 8 minutes where the draft recommends at most 24 hours, and uses the payment scheme's own key store.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-108 clm-visa-tap-109 clm-visa-tap-111 clm-visa-tap-112 clm-visa-tap-113" /> TAP launched almost eleven months before the draft, so the draft is not its basis.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-106" /> UCP's own RFC 9421 signing, with `Content-Digest` and an optional Web Bot Auth profile, sits closer to the draft than TAP does, and the UCP specification never names TAP.<Cite ids="clm-visa-tap-194 clm-visa-tap-195" />

With Stripe MPP, TAP is an optional layer.
