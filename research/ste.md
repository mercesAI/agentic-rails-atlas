## 2. Paraphrased rule summary for writers

This is an original summary of the public rule topics. Rule numbers come from the TechScribe public rule index for Issue 9 [src-ste-techscribe-rules9], a secondary source that paraphrases rule titles; the official numbering was not checked against the standard itself and is marked "reported". Nothing here reproduces rule text.

Section structure (reported, 9 sections): 1 Words, 2 Noun clusters (multi-word nouns), 3 Verbs, 4 Sentences, 5 Procedures, 6 Descriptive writing, 7 Safety instructions, 8 Punctuation and word counts, 9 Writing practices.

### 2.1 Words (section 1)

- Use approved words with their approved meaning and approved part of speech only (reported rules 1.1 to 1.3). Use only the approved verb and adjective forms (1.4).
- Terms that belong to your industry or project are technical nouns (TN) or technical verbs (TV) (1.5, 1.12). A word that is not approved is acceptable only when it is a TN or part of a TN (1.6). Keep this use limited (FAQ).
- One TN for one thing; do not switch between synonyms (1.11). Do not use a TN as a verb or a TV as a noun (1.7, 1.13).
- Prefer TNs that are short, standard in your field, and not slang or jargon (1.8 to 1.10).
- American English spelling unless another directive applies (1.14).

### 2.2 Noun clusters (section 2)

- Multi-word nouns: no more than three nouns in a row (2.1).
- When a TN is longer than three words, write it in full the first time, then shorten it, for example with a shorter name or an abbreviation that you define (2.2).

### 2.3 Verbs (section 3)

- Only the verb forms the dictionary gives (3.1). Allowed tenses and forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, and the past participle used as an adjective (3.2, 3.3).
- No complex verb constructions made with auxiliaries: no perfect tenses, no progressive forms, no modal stacks (3.4).
- The -ing form appears only as a TN or inside a TN (3.5). The FAQ adds that some dictionary words end in -ing but are nouns, adjectives, pronouns or prepositions (its examples are of the kind "opening", "remaining", "something", "during").
- Active voice. In descriptive text the passive is allowed only when the agent is unknown (3.6, FAQ). Procedures never use the passive.
- Express an action with a verb, not with a noun built from the verb (3.7).

### 2.4 Sentences (section 4)

- Short and clear sentences (4.1). Do not drop words and do not use contractions to save space (4.2).
- Complex text goes into a vertical list (4.3). Use connecting words to link related sentences (4.4). Use articles or demonstrative adjectives before nouns (4.5).

### 2.5 Procedures (section 5)

- Procedural sentences: maximum 20 words (5.1; the 20-word limit is reported by all tools studied and by Wikipedia, not read in the standard).
- One instruction per sentence, unless two or more actions happen at the same time (5.2).
- Instructions in the imperative (5.3).
- When a condition or descriptive statement comes before the command, write it first and separate it from the command with a comma (5.4). The FAQ confirms the condition-first order.
- Notes give information only, never instructions (5.5).

### 2.6 Descriptive writing (section 6)

- Descriptive sentences: maximum 25 words (reported by tools and Wikipedia).
- One topic per paragraph; the first sentence is the topic sentence; at most six sentences per paragraph (reported; the Tools page confirms that a checker cannot judge the topic sentence).

### 2.7 Safety instructions (section 7)

- Start with a word that identifies the level of risk (7.1), then a clear and accurate command or condition (7.2), then an explanation of the risk or possible result (7.3).
- Atlas convention (ours, not the standard): WARNING only for risk to people (rare in this domain), CAUTION for risk of financial loss, data exposure or security compromise, NOTE for information. The FAQ notes that company legal departments often control safety wording.

### 2.8 Punctuation and word counts (section 8)

- All standard punctuation except the semicolon (8.1). Hyphens join directly related words (8.2). Parentheses are allowed (8.3).
- Counting conventions (reported from the TechScribe index): in a vertical list a colon counts like a full stop (8.4); text in parentheses counts as one word (8.5); several element types each count as one word (8.6; the public index truncates the list, and Ste100Mark's authors read it as numbers with units, abbreviations, identifiers and quoted text); a hyphenated word counts as one word (8.7).

### 2.9 Writing practices (section 9)

- When a one-for-one word swap does not work, restructure the sentence (9.1). Use each approved word correctly (9.2). No phrasal verbs (9.3). Keep a consistent style and terminology (9.4).

## 3. Linter design notes (`src/lib/ste/`, `pnpm lint:ste`)

Design goals: deterministic, offline, no dictionary content in the repository, two text modes (procedure, descriptive), JSON report per protocol plus CLI summary, build fails on hard limits (procedural sentence over 20 words, descriptive over 25 words). Label every report: "Written to the public ASD-STE100 Issue 9 writing rules. Vocabulary not checked against the official dictionary."

### 3.1 Segmentation that keeps code spans, URLs and field names as single tokens

Confirmed behavior of compromise 14.18.0 (run in this project on 2026-10-10): the tokenizer keeps `https://api.example.com/v1/pay` as one term tagged `#Url`, keeps `payment_requirements.maxAmountRequired` as one term, keeps inline backtick spans inside one sentence, does not split on "e.g.", and `.wordCount()` counts a URL as one word. It splits hyphenated identifiers: `SD-JWT` becomes `SD-` and `JWT`, `EIP-712` becomes `EIP-` and `712`. It also expands contractions internally, so `match('#Contraction')` returned nothing for "Don't".

Approach, in order:

1. Markdown structure pass (own code). Remove fenced code blocks entirely. Treat headings, list items and table cells as separate segments. Blank lines delimit paragraphs. A numbered or bulleted step is one segment and is checked as a procedure sentence. Record the source line for each segment.
2. Protection pass (own code). Replace with opaque placeholder tokens, in this order: inline code spans, URLs and emails, markdown link targets (keep the label), field names and identifiers (tokens with a dot, underscore, slash or camelCase, such as `maxAmountRequired`, `X-PAYMENT`, `WWW-Authenticate`), hyphenated identifiers from the technical-noun list (such as `SD-JWT`, `EIP-712`, `ERC-8004`, `3-D Secure`), and multi-word technical nouns from the seed list (longest match first). Each placeholder counts as one word, is tagged as a noun, and is never flagged by the -ing, passive or noun-cluster checks. Placeholders survive the compromise pass because they contain only letters (for example `TNQ007`); map them back for display.
3. Sentence pass. Run `nlp(segment, lexicon)` where `lexicon` maps placeholder tokens and single-word technical nouns to `'Noun'` (keys are matched case-insensitively, confirmed: `{ agentid: 'Noun' }` tagged `agentId`). Use `.sentences().out('array')` for sentence boundaries and `.sentences().json()` for offsets. Keep a fallback regex splitter `(?<=[.!?])["')\]]*\s+(?=[A-Z0-9"'(\`*_])` for text where compromise is not loaded (tests, CLI dry run). For vertical lists, treat a trailing colon as a sentence end (reported counting rule 8.4).
4. Word count. Count whitespace-separated tokens after protection; a hyphenated word is one token already; count a parenthetical as one token (reported rule 8.5); count a number plus unit as one token (reported rule 8.6).
5. Raw-text checks run on the unprotected segment text, not on compromise output: contractions, semicolons, em dashes, and the vague-modal list.

### 3.3 Passive-voice heuristic

Primary signal (confirmed in this project): `doc.verbs().json()` returns, for each verb phrase, `verb.grammar.form`; the values `passive-past`, `passive-present` and `passive-future` identify passives, including "has been updated" (`passive-present`) and "is computed" (`passive-present`). The `#Passive` tag is applied to each term in the construction, so `doc.match('#Passive')` locates the span.

Guard rails (learned from prior art):

- Stative participles after "is" or "are" (installed, connected, enabled, disabled, locked, closed, open, filled, signed) describe a state, which is allowed as a past participle used as an adjective. Do not flag them unless a "by" agent phrase follows (Ste100Mark approach) or the sentence is in a procedure and the participle is the main verb.
- In a procedure sentence that starts with an imperative, a later "is" plus participle inside a condition clause ("Make sure that the key is loaded") is a state check, not a passive instruction (Ste100Mark approach).
- Fallback regex when compromise is unavailable: a form of "be" followed by up to two words and then a word ending in -ed or -en, plus a short irregular participle list (ste-lint and danyuchn approach). Expect false positives on nouns ending in -ed such as "feed" and "speed" (JAICHANGPARK keeps an exclusion set).
- Severity: error in procedures, warning in descriptions. In descriptive text the writer can mark a sentence with a comment such as `<!-- ste-agent-unknown -->` to accept it.

### 3.4 -ing allowlist approach

Signal: `doc.match('#Gerund')` (confirmed: it returned "settling", "onboarding", "Signing" and did not return "string" or "nothing"). Add a suffix regex `\b[a-z][a-z-]{2,}ing\b` as a second pass for words compromise tagged as nouns, so that TN candidates are reported to the writer as "review".

Allowlist layers:

1. Dictionary-type exceptions that are not verbs: during, something, nothing, anything, everything, thing, string, ring, spring, building, opening, warning, setting (these are examples of ordinary English nouns and prepositions; the official dictionary was not consulted).
2. Technical-noun allowlist for this domain (Section 6 of this file): identity linking, key binding, onboarding, Lightning invoice, Lightning Network, signing key, settlement batching, streaming payment, routing hint, funding transaction, spending condition, landing page.
3. Protected placeholders never count (code spans, URLs, field names such as `pending`).

Anything else that ends in -ing is an error in both modes.

### 3.5 Noun-cluster detection with compromise

Confirmed: `doc.match('#Noun{4,}')` returned "payment channel settlement batch proof" and `doc.nouns().json()` gives each noun phrase with `noun.root`, `noun.determiner`, `noun.adjectives`, `noun.isPlural`. Method:

1. Protect multi-word technical nouns first; each becomes one placeholder noun, so "payment requirements object" counts as two nouns, not three.
2. Run `match('#Noun{4,}')` excluding `#Determiner`, `#Possessive`, `#Pronoun`, `#Value` and `#Url`.
3. Report the cluster, its length, and the suggestion to insert a preposition or define a shorter TN. Severity warning (the standard's limit is reported as three nouns; proper names and acronyms inflate counts, so keep it advisory).
4. Fallback without POS: consecutive tokens not in a stop list, not ending in -ly or -ed, not numeric (JAICHANGPARK approach) or four consecutive Penn NN/NNS/NNP tags (Vale STE style approach, available through `.compute('penn')`).

### 3.6 Imperative-start check for procedure steps

Confirmed: `nlp('Sign the payload.').verbs().isImperative()` returns the verb, and `has('^#Imperative')` is true for "Sign the payload.", "Open the door.", "Set the header." and "Verify the signature before you settle.". Two cases need handling:

- Negative imperative: "Do not sign the payment." returns "Do not sign" from `.verbs().isImperative()` but `has('^#Imperative')` is false because the sentence starts with "Do". Accept a step whose first verb phrase is imperative, or whose text starts with "Do not".
- Condition first (reported rule 5.4): "If the address is wrong, stop the payment." returns nothing from `isImperative()`. Split with `.clauses()` (confirmed: it returns "If the address is wrong," and "stop the payment and report it."), skip leading clauses that start with `#Condition` (if, when, before, after, unless), and test the first remaining clause with `nlp(clause).verbs().first().has('#Imperative')`.
- Fallback: compare the first token with `nlp(firstToken).verbs().toInfinitive().text()`; equality plus a following `#Determiner` or `#Noun` is a weak imperative signal (DotDebian and Ste100Mark keep a hand list of lead verbs; ours can derive one from the protocol procedures as they are written).

### 3.7 Phrasal-verb list source

- Runtime: compromise tags phrasal verbs, confirmed `match('#PhrasalVerb #Particle')` returns "Set up" and `match('#PhrasalVerb')` returns the parts of "carry out". Compromise's list is `data/lexicon/verbs/phrasals.js`, 945 entries, MIT, credited to allmyphrasalverbs.com [src-compromise-phrasals].
- Static list for the fallback: a curated subset relevant to this domain (set up, carry out, find out, fill in, fill out, turn on, turn off, shut down, look up, log in, log out, sign in, sign up, roll back, spin up, tear down, hand off, opt in, opt out, pass through, point to, back up) with one approved alternative each (install or configure, do, find, complete, start, stop, close, find, connect, disconnect, authenticate, register, revert, start, remove, transfer, accept, refuse, send, refer to, copy). Store it in `src/lib/ste/phrasal-verbs.json` with a `source` field.

### 3.8 Vague modals

The standard allows a limited set of verb forms; which modal verbs are approved depends on the dictionary, which this project does not reproduce. The tools studied (OneRead, SimpleEnglish, 0xpili) all flag should, would, may, might and could, and keep must, can and will. Our linter flags should, would, may, might, could as "review" with the message to state the condition or the requirement instead (for example "The server returns 402 if the payment is missing" instead of "The server may return 402"). Confirmed: `match('#Modal')` returns the modal tokens.

### 3.9 Output

Per protocol: `{ protocol, mode counts, sentences, maxSentenceWords, paragraphs, findings: [{ ruleKey, severity, line, excerpt, suggestion }], label }`. CLI prints a table per file and exits non-zero on any error-severity finding. The UI reads the JSON for the "STE check report" block.

Common lessons across the tools:

1. None of them ships the official dictionary legitimately; the ones that include word lists or the PDF are the ones to avoid as sources. Keep vocabulary checking optional and local.
2. Regex passives are noisy on stative participles; POS-based or form-based detection (spaCy dependencies, wink POS, compromise `grammar.form`) plus a stative allowlist is the pattern that works.
3. Every tool collapses code spans, URLs, numbers with units and parentheticals to one word before counting.
4. Every serious tool separates "official rule" findings from "advisory heuristic" findings and from "house style" findings. Our report should keep that three-way classification.
5. Rule numbers differ between tools; cite the public TechScribe index and mark numbers as reported.

## 5. compromise API cheatsheet (version 14.18.0, confirmed)

Package facts (as of 2026-10-10): npm `compromise` latest 14.18.0 published 2026-10-05, license MIT, 14.0.0 published 2022-03-22, last-month downloads 4,250,818 (2026-09-10 to 2026-10-09), GitHub spencermountain/compromise 12,161 stars, default branch master, pushed 2026-10-09. Package exports `compromise` (three), `compromise/one`, `compromise/two`, `compromise/three`, `compromise/tokenize`; TypeScript types in `types/three.d.ts`. [src-compromise-repo], [src-compromise-npm], [src-compromise-npm-downloads]

Method names below were read in `docs/api.md` or the source and then executed in this project's installed copy on 2026-10-10 (the probe results are quoted in Section 3).

Construction and tokenizing

- `nlp(text)` returns a View of the whole document. `nlp(text, lexicon)` accepts a lexicon object such as `{ x402: 'Noun', agentid: 'Noun' }`; keys are matched case-insensitively (confirmed). [src-compromise-api-docs]
- `nlp.tokenize(text, lexicon?)` tokenizes without tagging; `nlp.plugin(plugin)`, `nlp.addWords(words, isFrozen?)`, `nlp.addTags(tags)`, `nlp.version`, `nlp.verbose(bool)`. [src-compromise-api-docs]

Views and selection (compromise/one and two)

- `.match(pattern)`, `.has(pattern)`, `.if(pattern)`, `.ifNo(pattern)`, `.not(pattern)`, `.before(pattern)`, `.after(pattern)`, `.lookBehind()`, `.lookAhead()`, `.splitOn()`, `.splitBefore()`, `.splitAfter()`, `.terms()`, `.fullSentences()`, `.wordCount()`, `.found`, `.length`, `.compute(name)`, `.first()`, `.eq(n)`.
- Output: `.text(format?)`, `.json(options)` (for example `.json({ terms: { tags: true } })` or `.json({ terms: { penn: true } })` after `.compute('penn')`), `.out('array')`, `.debug()`.
- Match syntax for tags: `#Noun{4,}` for a run of four or more nouns, `^#Imperative` for sentence-initial, `#PhrasalVerb #Particle`, `(#Past|#Part)`.

Sentences (compromise/three)

- `.sentences(n?)` returns the Sentences view; `.out('array')` gives the strings. Sentences methods: `.json()`, `.toPastTense()`, `.toPresentTense()`, `.toFutureTense()`, `.toInfinitive()`, `.toNegative()`, `.toPositive()`, `.isQuestion()`, `.isExclamation()`, `.isStatement()`. Aliases `.toPast()`, `.toPresent()`, `.toFuture()`.
- `.clauses()` splits at clause boundaries (confirmed on a conditional sentence). `.chunks()` splits noun and verb phrases.

Verbs

- `.verbs(n?)` returns the Verbs view. Methods confirmed in `src/3-three/verbs/api/api.js`: `.parse()`, `.json()`, `.subjects()`, `.adverbs()`, `.isSingular()`, `.isPlural()`, `.isImperative()`, `.toInfinitive()`, `.toPresentTense()`, `.toPastTense()`, `.toFutureTense()`, `.toGerund()`, `.toPastParticiple()`, `.conjugate()`, `.isNegative()`, `.isPositive()`, `.toPositive()`, `.toNegative()`; aliases `.toPast()`, `.toPresent()`, `.toFuture()`.
- `.verbs().json()[i].verb` has `root`, `preAdverbs`, `postAdverbs`, `auxiliary`, `negative`, `prefix`, `infinitive`, `grammar`. `grammar` has `form`, `tense` (`PastTense` or `PresentTense`), `copula`, `isInfinitive`. Confirmed `form` values in `parse/grammar/forms.js`: `imperative`, `infinitive`, `simple-present`, `simple-past`, `simple-future`, `present-progressive`, `past-progressive`, `future-progressive`, `present-perfect`, `past-perfect`, `future-perfect`, `present-perfect-progressive`, `past-perfect-progressive`, `future-perfect-progressive`, `passive-present`, `passive-past`, `passive-future`, `present-conditional`, `past-conditional`, `gerund-phrase`, `want-infinitive`. [src-compromise-verbs-forms]
- `.verbs().conjugate()` returns objects with `Infinitive`, `PastTense`, `PresentTense`, `Gerund`, `FutureTense` (confirmed on "sign").
- There is no `isPassive()` method on the Verbs view in 14.18.0; use `grammar.form` or the `#Passive` tag.

Nouns

- `.nouns(n?)` returns noun phrases including determiners. Methods in `src/3-three/nouns/api/api.js`: `.parse()`, `.json()`, `.conjugate()`, `.isPlural()`, `.isSingular()`, `.adjectives()`, `.toPlural()`, `.toSingular()`. `.nouns().json()[i].noun` has `root`, `number`, `determiner`, `adjectives`, `isPlural`, `isSubordinate`.

Tags relevant to the linter (from `docs/tag-definitions.md`) [src-compromise-tag-defs]

- Verbs: `#Verb`, `#Infinitive`, `#Imperative`, `#Gerund`, `#PastTense`, `#Participle`, `#PresentTense`, `#FutureTense`, `#Copula`, `#Auxiliary`, `#Modal`, `#Passive`, `#PhrasalVerb`, `#Particle`, `#Negative`.
- Nouns: `#Noun`, `#Singular`, `#Plural`, `#ProperNoun`, `#Possessive`, `#Pronoun`, `#Acronym`, `#Activity`, `#Actor`, `#Uncountable`, `#Unit`.
- Closed class and other: `#Determiner`, `#Preposition`, `#Conjunction`, `#Condition` (if), `#Connector`, `#Adverb`, `#Adjective`, `#Hyphenated`, `#Url`, `#Email`, `#Abbreviation`, `#Value`, `#Cardinal`.

Penn tags

- `doc.compute('penn')` sets `term.penn`; read with `.json({ terms: { penn: true } })`. Mapping in `src/2-two/preTagger/compute/penn.js`: Gerund VBG, PastTense VBD, Participle VBN, PresentTense VBZ, Infinitive VB, Modal and Auxiliary MD, Particle RP, Determiner DT, Preposition IN, Adverb RB, Adjective JJ. Confirmed output: "The agent signed the payload quickly" gives DT NN VBD DT NN RB.

Imperative tagging internals

- `src/2-two/preTagger/compute/tagger/3rd-pass/08-imperative.js` tags a sentence-initial infinitive or Noun|Verb switch word as `#Imperative` when a noun or determiner follows and no other verb appears soon after, with special cases for "go to" and "keep it". Compromise's own tests include short commands ("shoot the ball"), and the probe confirmed three-word imperatives. [src-compromise-imperative]

Sentence splitting internals

- `src/1-one/tokenize/methods/01-sentences/`: a punctuation-run splitter, then merge passes for abbreviations (lists in `tokenize/model/abbreviations/`), acronyms such as "F.B.I.", leading initials, ellipses, quotes and parentheses. `tokenize/methods/web.js` recognizes emails, explicit URLs and bare domains so they stay one token. [src-compromise-web-tokens]

Caveats found by the probe

- Hyphenated identifiers such as `SD-JWT` and `EIP-712` are split into two terms; protect them before tagging.
- `match('#Contraction')` returned nothing for "Don't": detect contractions on raw text.
- The `#Passive` tag is on each term of the construction, so a passive span is `match('#Passive+')`.

## 6. Technical-noun seed list for this domain

Drawn from ATLAS_PROMPT.md Appendix C plus the eight protocol names. These are allowed as technical nouns (TN) in the linter's allowlist; multi-word entries are protected as one token before counting and tagging; hyphenated identifiers are protected so the tokenizer does not split them. Entries marked (-ing) also feed the -ing allowlist. Count: 150 entries.

Protocol and program names (12): x402, ERC-8004, Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, Agentic Commerce Protocol, Universal Commerce Protocol, Machine Payments Protocol, Agent Payments Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Visa Token Service, Web Bot Auth, Verifiable Intent

ERC-8004 and on-chain (18): agent card, agentId, agentURI, agentWallet, registration file, feedback file, validation request, CAIP-2, CAIP-10, CAIP-122, EIP-712, EIP-3009, ERC-1271, ERC-721, Permit2, UUPS proxy, reputation system, Sybil attack

MPP and Tempo (14): challenge, credential, intent, charge, session, subscription, voucher, TIP-20, Tempo, Shared Payment Token, delegated payment, allowance, vault token, stablecoin

ACP and UCP (16): checkout session, line item, minor units, idempotency key, capability, extension, payment handler, merchant of record, continue URL, escalation, business profile, UCP-Agent header, identity linking (-ing), Agent Commerce Protocol, Universal Cart, delegated payment token

AP2 and credentials (14): mandate, open mandate, closed mandate, constraint, Trusted Surface, credential provider, merchant payment processor, SD-JWT, key binding (-ing), cnf claim, OpenID4VP, Digital Credentials API, verifiable credential, DID

HTTP signatures and TAP (12): RFC 9421, signature base, covered components, keyid, nonce, tag, Signature-Agent, JWKS, consumer recognition object, payment container, ID token, content digest

Agent ecosystems and Lightning (12): A2A, MCP, AAIF, OASF, ANS, NANDA, L402, macaroon, Lightning invoice (-ing), Lightning Network (-ing), preimage, USDC

Rules for the list: one TN per concept (for example use "facilitator", never "settlement service" for the same role); do not use a TN as a verb ("to x402 a request" is not allowed); write a TN longer than three words in full on first use, then use the short form (for example "Shared Payment Token (SPT)"). The -ing entries are allowed only as these nouns; "signing" as a verb form remains an error.
