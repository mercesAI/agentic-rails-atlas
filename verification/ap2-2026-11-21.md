## What changed outside the site
AP2's repository did not move: `main` is still e1ea56d (2026-04-29), and v0.2.0 is still the latest release. What moved was Google's product statement and outside reports on the code:
- 2026-11-14: a third party opened AP2 issue 398, reporting three ways user constraints can slip between roles in the v0.2 Python samples: Payment-side constraints that no verifier claims, mixed mandate pairs checked by different roles, and budget and recurrence counters kept only by the agent. The site gives it as reported and has not reproduced it.
- 2026-11-17: Google's Keyword post "Put Gemini to work for you" (Josh Woodward) says, after an example checkout through Gemini's new Google Pay integration, that Gemini now uses AP2 to make purchases like this. The Gemini release notes roll out checkout with Google Pay in Tasks in Gemini in English on web, Android and iOS to US users aged 18 and over, with a single-use virtual card after a 1-tap and device approval. Google's purchases help page (undated, read 2026-11-21) lets the user set the allowed merchants, a budget of up to $1000 and an expiry within 14 days, keeps Purchase confirmation on by default but optional, and says Gemini uses a browser and may pay with a virtual card number. Its Tasks help says Spark was renamed Task, so Google's 2026-05-19 promise of AP2 "starting with Gemini Spark" concerns the same product. Google names American Express (coming soon), Mastercard and Visa for digital tokens on supported cards without saying that the tokens or the networks take part in AP2, and it publishes no AP2 version, mandate types, signer or verifier; neither the release notes nor Google's Gemini help pages name AP2. The post also says Google will integrate UCP into Gemini next, and Google's UCP platform profiles declare no AP2 extension. The same day Google Wallet reached iOS in the US, and Google says Wallet will also be a hub for managing agentic purchases across Google. This overtook the dated statements the site carried: the production metric ("none found", as of 2026-10-21), the STE note (correct on 2026-10-21), the two scenarios' AP2 sentences (as of 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21), the Gemini flow's step detail and the autonomous-restock flow summary (both as of 2026-10-11) and UCP's claim 197 (as of 2026-10-20).
- 2026-11-17: a third party opened AP2 issue 399 with fix PR 400. CheckoutMandateChain.verify() in the reference Python SDK compares `checkout_hash` only when the caller passes `expected_checkout_hash`, and the repository's own tests use `checkout_hash='hash'`, so a caller that copies them accepts a swapped `checkout_jwt` that meets the open mandate's constraints. The code at e1ea56d matches the report, and PaymentMandateChain.verify() treats `transaction_id` the same way (the site's reading of the code). On 2026-11-21 both were open with no maintainer response, advisory or CVE. It is a weakness of the reference code, not of the specification, which requires the merchant to compare the hash, and no source says Gemini or any deployment uses this SDK.
- 2026-11-20: ethereum/ERCs PR #2191 (commit 9912307) rewrote ERC-8226, which stays a Draft: mandates are now keyed by agent, principal and target contract, and the per-transaction and cumulative caps sit in per-asset limits that all of a mandate's actions share. Claim 197 (as of 2026-10-11) had listed those caps among the mandate's fields; it now gives the new structure. ERC-8226 still does not mention AP2.
- 2026-11-20: UCP maintainers closed PR #540, an AP2 mandate-evaluation extension in a third-party namespace, unmerged; UCP's core design for AP2 mandate evaluation and receipts stays in Enhancement Proposal #515. Not on the site (UCP-06, deferred as minor).
- 2026-11-11, before the window: the European Commission's Digital Fairness Act proposal (COM(2026) 1100). Its Article 26 would require a business that offers consumers an automated system that concludes contracts for them to have the consumer choose at first use whether to confirm every contract, only some under criteria the consumer sets, or none; the system must also disclose any payment the business receives for proposing a contract, or any conflict of interest, and then have the consumer confirm that contract whatever the earlier choice. Article 26 does not apply to the provision of financial services. It is a proposal, not law, at the preparatory stage in Parliament (2026-11-21), and the compliance page has a new EU liability row for it.
- Not on the site: the W3C and GS1 e-commerce workshop's draft report (first committed 2026-11-10, last modified 2026-11-17 on w3.org) summarizes a FIDO Payments Technical Working Group talk on harmonizing AP2, Verifiable Intent and Digital Payment Credentials around a credential provider and a deterministic trusted surface. It describes a talk, not a published FIDO deliverable (UCP-08, deferred as minor).
